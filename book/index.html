I · The Ember Gate

Rowan had never meant to open the gate. It simply opened for him, the way old doors do when they recognize a hand descended from the ones who built them — reluctantly, and with a great deal of dust. He had only leaned against it to catch his breath, and the iron had gone warm under his palm like something waking from a long sleep.

Beyond it, the wards flickered like breath on cold glass, and somewhere far below the hill, something that had waited a very long time finally began to stir. He told himself it was the wind. He told himself that twice more before he stopped believing it.

By the time he reached the bottom of the hill, the lanterns of Ashbourne were already lit, small and stubborn against the dark, and none of them knew yet what he had let through.

II · The Wolf and the Crown

The letter came on the back of a hawk with a temper, and by the time Mireille had wrestled it free of the bird's talons, half the wax seal had already cracked. It bore no name, only a pressed sprig of rowan and a single line: they have opened the gate.

She read it twice standing in the kitchen doorway, once more sitting down, and did not notice the kettle screaming until her mother came in and lifted it herself. "Who's it from?" her mother asked, and Mireille, who had spent six years pretending she didn't remember the old stories, found she remembered every one of them at once.

By morning the road to Ashbourne was already busy with things that were not, strictly speaking, travelers.

III · What the Old Maps Hide

The librarian at Marrow Hall had told them, more than once, that there was no book by that name — and each time she said it, she said it a little too quickly, and glanced just slightly toward the locked case beneath the west window.

It was Rowan who finally asked her outright, and it was the ember-mark on his wrist, newly raised and still tender, that made her set down her pen. "You'd best come after hours, then," she said. "And bring the girl. Some doors only open for two."

What they found beneath the floorboards that night was not, in the end, a book at all.
